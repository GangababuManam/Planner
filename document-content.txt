102: PAGE 3 | THREAT EXPOSURE MANAGEMENT
103: You Don't Have a Vulnerability Problem.
You Have an Exposure Problem.
104: Reduce Exposure. Build Resilience.
105: Enterprises already know they have vulnerabilities—often thousands or millions of findings across increasingly complex environments.
106: The real challenge is understanding which weaknesses can become meaningful business risk, how attackers could use them, and what security teams should do first.
107: Move from Findings to Exposure Intelligence
108: ANTRIQ brings vulnerability, asset, threat, identity, attack-path and business context together to help security teams move from managing findings to continuously managing exposure.
109: Discover → Contextualise → Prioritise → Remediate → Validate
110: Discover the Real Attack Surface
111: Continuously understand the assets, vulnerabilities, misconfigurations and security weaknesses that contribute to enterprise exposure.
112: Cloud and infrastructure
113: Applications and APIs
114: Endpoints and workloads
115: Identity and privilege
116: External attack surface
117: Security controls and configuration
118: AI infrastructure and intelligent systems
119: Third-party and technology dependencies
120: Context Changes Everything
121: A critical vulnerability does not automatically equal critical business risk. ANTRIQ enriches findings with the context required to understand actual exposure.
122: Vulnerability severity
123: + Exploitability
124: + Active threat intelligence
125: + Asset criticality
126: + Identity and privilege
127: + Attack paths
128: + Existing security controls
129: + Business impact
130: = Exposure Intelligence
131: 100,000 Findings Don't Need 100,000 Actions.
132: The objective is not to make every finding look urgent. It is to identify the small number of actions that materially reduce risk.
133: 100,000 Security Findings → 2,500 Relevant Exposures → 180 Material Risks → 15 Priority Actions
134: The numbers above are illustrative website storytelling, not performance claims. They should be presented visually as an example of the prioritisation concept.
135: Prioritise What Attackers Can Actually Use
136: Traditional vulnerability management often asks, “How severe is this vulnerability?” Intelligent exposure management asks a broader question: “Can it be exploited, what can an attacker reach, what business capability is at risk, and what should we do now?”
137: Severity → Context
138: Findings → Exposure
139: Volume → Priority
140: Reactive patching → Risk reduction
141: From Dashboards to Decisions

143: What is our current exposure?
144: Where are our highest-risk attack paths?
145: Which vulnerabilities are realistically exploitable?
146: Which assets and identities matter most?
147: What should we remediate first?
148: Which action will reduce the most risk?
149: Did remediation actually reduce exposure?
150: Reduce Exposure.
151: The objective is not to fix every vulnerability. It is to continuously reduce the exposure that matters—using intelligence to focus people, time and investment where they create the greatest security outcome.
